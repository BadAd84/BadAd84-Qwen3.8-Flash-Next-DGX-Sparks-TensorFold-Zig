# C. Where the remaining speed is

## C.1 Cross-check of the two load-bearing comparisons

For production's 87.6 and 412.8 chat tok/s, I used the raw per-cell file `work/SyncBench/box/out/window/P1/conc_t0.json` (87.6 at streams 1, 412.8 at streams 8) and independently `work/SyncBench/box/facts2.txt:254,258`, which repeats both values and the per-stream medians. For main's 41.7, I used `work/SyncBench/box/out/window/M1/a/conc_t0.json` and the same `facts2.txt`. For main's 260.3, I used `work/RoGpu/scripts/box/out/run/1/M1/conc_t0.json` and independently `work/RoGpu/receipt/summary.json` (also `receipt/cells.csv`).

The ratios are:

- One stream: `87.6 / 41.7 = 2.1007`; production is 110.1 percent faster, and main is `41.7 / 87.6 = 47.60 percent` of production.
- Eight streams: `412.8 / 260.3 = 1.5859`; production is 58.6 percent faster, and main is `260.3 / 412.8 = 63.06 percent` of production.

These are not one confound-free window. The 260.3 cell is from the later read-out window's main `46c6ee229`, while production and the 41.7 cell are from the sync window. In the sync window itself, main's eight-stream cell is 257.4, so `412.8 / 257.4 = 1.6037` and main is 62.36 percent of production (`work/SyncBench/SUMMARY.md`, chat table). The gap is large either way; the third decimal is not portable across boots.

## C.2 What can be assigned to checkpoint, engine and unknown

The only clean chain is on the older 1.2.7 engine. `P` is production on INT4, `I` is our engine on the same INT4 checkpoint, and `F` is the same engine on FP4, from `docs/INT4-PORT.md`, phase 3. For each cell:

- total ratio = `P / F`
- engine ratio = `P / I`
- checkpoint ratio = `I / F`
- log engine share = `ln(P/I) / ln(P/F)`; log checkpoint share = `ln(I/F) / ln(P/F)`

The three ratios multiply to one by construction. The shares describe this chain, not independent causal experiments and not current main. The source triage also flags the 6 and 8 stream cross-checkpoint pairs as not like-for-like: the FP4 cells used a 131,072 window and BF16 drafter linears (`work/upsubmit/Triage/int4.md`, section 7.5). Those rows are arithmetic on the available records, and the experiment below is required before using them as a deployment estimate.

Mechanism for the checkpoint share: production routes 5 experts a token against FP4's 10, and its LM head is int4 against BF16. Mechanism for the engine share: on the same INT4 checkpoint, the older engine still trails production. The newer same-checkpoint comparison reads `65.0/122.2` versus `69.4/125.4` at one stream and `195.6/334.5` versus `239.8/384.1` at eight, or ratios 0.937/0.975 and 0.816/0.871 (`docs/PROD-VS-ZIG-2026-11-18.md`, first results table). **Unknown:** there is no current-main INT4 admission series on `1ece6e6db`, and production does not provide a crossed production-engine-on-FP4 arm. Therefore the current 2.10x and 1.59x gaps cannot be exactly partitioned. The cheapest settling experiment is a two-by-two as far as possible: current main+FP4, current main+INT4 port, production+INT4, at 1, 2, 4, 6 and 8, with tokens/round, ms/round and expert/head bytes. A fully crossed fourth arm does not exist and should be named, not inferred away.

## C.3 Main's measured improvement and cold prefill

Main is level with the old base in all ten decode cells (`work/SyncBench/SUMMARY.md`, verdict table). Cold TTFT is faster in all ten. Redoing two examples from its table:

- 32k off: `1 - mean(15.043,15.016) / mean(16.064,16.074) = 6.54 percent`.
- 64k off: `1 - mean(30.750,30.744) / mean(33.147,33.142) = 7.23 percent`.
- 2k on: `1 - mean(0.984,0.993) / mean(1.118,1.096) = 10.68 percent`.

The full measured range is 6.5 to 10.7 percent. The boot lines also change from 1,536 to 3,072 prompt rows, making #3726 the first bisect candidate, not a proven attribution (`work/SyncBench/SUMMARY.md`, section 8). Production's cold 32k is about `10.53 / 15.03 = 70.1 percent` of main's TTFT, and cold 64k about `21.65 / 30.75 = 70.4 percent`, but that is cross-checkpoint and includes the engine difference.

Two already measured prompt improvements are immediately relevant:

- `READOUT_FUSED=1` gives 3.19 to 6.47 percent cold TTFT improvement in the knob window (`work/KnobTest/RESULT.md`, window 1), and 8 of 8 cold comparisons faster in the later default-flip window, with decode level and 792/792 compared replies equal (`work/RoGpu/RESULT.md`, cold and exactness).
- Solo chunk 16,384 adds another 1.12 to 1.39 percent at 32k/64k over the unset 8,192 default, with 2k/8k level (`work/KnobTest/RESULT.md`, headline and `VERDICTS.md`). It is not a universal default: the workload is long one-stream prompts.

# D. What not to chase

- **The relaxed MX128 or its GEMM2 form.** They change prompt sums and are forbidden upstream even opt-in. Only the exact re-cut is legitimate (`work/upsubmit/Triage/prompt.md`).
- **HC_Q12 as currently shaped, or more raw-block prefetch alone.** It has no served win and costs 1.02 GiB; escape prefetch does not solve the resident-copy problem.
- **Environment round prices.** Three price lines produce the same decisions. Main's learned pricing is the replacement.
- **Looser FP4 draft confidence.** `-0.15` and `-0.25` lose on the measured FP4 cells; the production INT4 setting does not transfer (`patches/1.2.7/README.md`, draft settings; `docs/FP4-PORT-AND-RETEST.md`, section 1).
- **The standalone O_DIRECT row cache and `GATHER_DIRECT` knob.** Buffered only ties plain. Change the read shape or stop.
- **Generic graph capture, cp.async expert rings and tile micro-optimisation.** The earlier graph lever was flat, the NVFP4 ring keeps GB10 on its old register form, and the hot qmm/expert work is at or near measured bandwidth (`docs/FP4-PORT-AND-RETEST.md`, section 6.7; `work/SyncBench/SURVEY.md`, A1).
- **Cache-eviction tuning on the present resume trace.** The knob receipt kept 8,598 tokens under both policies, so there was no victim for the policy to choose (`work/KnobTest/VERDICTS.md`, `TF_CACHE_EVICT`).
- **More ramp/miss/shared copy knobs.** They returned the default counters to the digit. Resync must first be shown to fire on a real edit.
- **Changing top-k or accepting another lossy served format.** That changes model behaviour and violates upstream's rules. INT4 is a checkpoint choice with measured comparable quality, not permission to degrade an existing checkpoint.
- **The request journal as a speed lever.** It is a diagnostic and its measured arm produced zero finish records (`work/SyncBench/SUMMARY.md`, section 1).

# F. Process judgement

The campaign is **not yet fit for purpose at its current GPU-hour cost**, despite unusually good raw evidence retention. The good parts are real: manifests and image ids, per-cell run files, explicit rejected arms, planted controls, restored production, and honest "not taken" labels. The later `RoGpu` dry-run covers 17 modes, including `argcheck_nomarker`, and is the right kind of correction (`work/RoGpu/GATES.md`). But the failures are no longer isolated mistakes: they are repeated instances of three missing system boundaries. Throughput per GPU hour will improve more from closing those boundaries than from another kernel arm.

## 1. Make one scheduler own the GPU and every serve lifecycle transition

Every GPU job, queued retry, production restart and helper launcher must acquire the same lease; `LOCK_HOLDER=0` and ad hoc production scripts cannot bypass it. The lease owner also verifies the running image immediately before each probe.

Justification:

- P5: "a foreign `start.sh` ran while this window held the lock", followed by all six decode arms failing (`work/P5Gpu/CLOSED.md`).
- Journal: both ON boots hit "`START-LOCK on: another start.sh holds the recipe's start.lock`" and produced zero finish records (`work/SyncBench/SUMMARY.md`, section 1).
- The lock is not sufficient by itself: two later records show different drivers queued or active around the same serve lifecycle (`work/Fuse0018Fix/RESULT.md`, final lock holders; `work/RoGpu/RESULT.md`, final lock holders).

This is the largest expected process win because a single foreign start invalidates a whole matrix, not one cell.

## 2. Run an executable preflight contract before any build, covering admission, settings and marker/provenance gates

Preflight the exact launch matrix and each control arm's unsupported settings before building images. Dry-run the unmodified driver with synthetic pass/fail fixtures for every marker, image and analyzer. Include an nvcc registration build whenever a CUDA contract changes.

Justification:

- The file-versus-line `ARGCHECK-OK` bug cost a complete 37-minute build before any arm (`work/RoGpu/WATCH.md`).
- All eight first sync arms failed admission after the build (`work/SyncBench/SUMMARY.md`, section 1).
- The bisect's k=0 control used fp8 on main and the ladder never ran (`work/SeriesBisect/RESULT.md`, section 4).
- `run_sync.sh` ran an image whose marker check had already failed (`work/SyncBench/SUMMARY.md`, section 2).
- The host-only contract gate could not analyse registrations; an nvcc build later caught the real failure (`work/Fuse0018Fix/GATES.md`).

## 3. Make evidence schemas fail closed, and reuse validated build artifacts

A speed table may be emitted only when every required cell has its resume status, nonempty token hashes, warm cached-token proof where applicable, the counters needed for attribution, and the declared repeats. Missing evidence is a driver failure before the next arm, not a post-processing footnote. Keep validated images and the contract runner; never delete the artifact before its existence check.

Justification:

- Golden had `token_sha256: []`, concurrent hashes above one stream were absent, and the warm boot made every sample `NOTWARM` (`work/SyncBench/SUMMARY.md`, sections 5, 6 and 11).
- `mtp_rounds_total` was absent, so the intended draft-yield attribution could not be computed (`work/SyncBench/SUMMARY.md`, section 8).
- `SeriesFix` declares no rerun pass and moves two main-readable settings on its M arm (`work/SeriesFix/RESULT.md`, section 6).
- `RoGpu` deletes the contract runner before testing whether it can be reused, forcing a 22-minute rebuild (`work/RoGpu/GATES.md`, finding 3).

These changes do not relax the noise rule or exactness requirements. They move failures to the cheapest possible point: before a build, before a GPU hold, and before an attractive but unqualified number can enter the campaign record.
