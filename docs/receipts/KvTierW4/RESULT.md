# KvTierW4: the fleet rule, the agent shape, the disk budget, and a crash

Window 4 of the persistent NVMe KV tier (`work/KvTierRecon/DESIGN.md`), on spark6 in one GPU-lock chain
on 2026-11-23, after windows 1 to 3 proved the write and the read (`work/KvTierW1`, `W2`, `W3`). Every
figure below is from this window's own arm directories (`~/work/KvTierW4/out/<arm>/`: boot logs, server
logs, `/stats` snapshots, `/v1/imprint/resume` probes, request ids and texts) or from the window log; all
of it is in `work/KvTierW4/box/w4-evidence.tgz`. `work/KvTierW4/tables.md`, generated by
`payload/analyse4.py` from that tree, carries every table including the per-stream and TTFT cells this
file summarises.

## 0. The shapes this image could and could not take (measured, before any cell)

The image is `tensorfold-qwen38:kv-w1`, id `sha256:ef6967ac57fb40a19f0c54c4788ac1e9f79fb73cf29b61ba3f19090a961108bb`,
labels `tf.patches 4144696f21e3` and `tf.kernels embedded`: the `patches/main-1ece6e6db-r5-kvtier`
series. Every boot used the production container's own environment (its 54 `TENSORFOLD_`/`TF_FLASHNEXT_`
variables, read from the running production container), 8 slots and a 262,144-token window where the base
admitted them, fp8 KV, `--dashboard` so the store's own counters are readable at `/stats`, and a 40 s
boot. Two refusals shaped the window:

1. **Production's checkpoint is refused by name.** Booting the image with production's INT4-AutoRound
   checkpoint (the one production serves) fails at load:
   `error: flashnext: config.json's quantization_config.quant_method differs from the supported Flash Next
   CUDA layout` and `error: UnsupportedCheckpoint`. Every measurement here is therefore on the NVIDIA NVFP4
   checkpoint the image takes, for which its boot line reads `model 74.34 GiB` at a 131,072 window and
   `model 77.11 GiB` at 262,144 (one slot). Production serves `azampatti/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-125B-A5B-INT4-AutoRound`
   with about 66 GiB of weights and its own cache line reads `6,361 MiB`.
2. **Eight streams at a 262,144-token window are refused by the base.** It reserves a whole window per
   stream (`zig/src/native/cuda_streams.zig:15,91`), so the NVFP4 boot refuses with
   `--parallel 8 needs 39.90 GiB for its streams at a 262144-token window, and the CUDA memory budget
   leaves 25.06 GiB: serve --parallel 5, or lower --context`. The production shape is possible on its own
   base, where the weights are smaller and the stream plan differs; this base also refuses the KV page
   pool beside the tier by name: "the `--learn-conversations` writes the host prompt cache's states, and
   TF_KV_SHARE keeps prompts as pool pages instead: serve with one of the two"
   (`zig/src/native/cuda.zig:342`).

What was run instead: the five concurrency levels at **8 slots and a 131,072-token window** (the largest
8-slot window this base admits: `8 streams at once, 2.68 GiB each at a 131072-token window (fp8 caches),
of 25.1 GiB left after a 10.0 GiB reserve; prompts in passes of up to 3072 rows, a 3.6 GiB prompt
cache`), and the **production window at 262,144 with 4 slots** for levels 1, 2 and 4 (`4 streams at once,
4.99 GiB each at a 262144-token window (fp8 caches), of 25.1 GiB left after a 10.0 GiB reserve; a 5.1
GiB prompt cache`). Six and eight streams at 262,144 are UNRUN and named in section 9, as is the
production checkpoint and therefore production's exact combination.

## 1. The fleet rule: every concurrent level, the tier on and off

Method: one fresh boot per arm per window, order OFF1 ON1 ON2 OFF2, two boots an arm; each boot runs the
five levels (or 1, 2, 4 at the production window) with the prose and code prompt sets of
`scripts/qwen38/zig/bench/draft_accounting.py`, a warm-up wave and three timed waves of 256 greedy tokens
a stream. Tier-on boots open a fresh store root per boot (no pre-existing state can resume); tier-off
boots pass no tier flag. The verdicts are the maintainer's noise rule: SLOWER when both ON boots are
under both OFF boots and the mean gap is over 1 percent, FASTER when both are over both and over 1
percent, LEVEL otherwise; for a latency, over means slower.

## 2. The agent shape: seven turns, each its own boot (the hour-apart resume)

The conversation is a 100,000-word reference text in the system message and seven short exchanges that
append: turn 1 seeds it, turns 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7 append, and turn 5 **rewrites the first message** (the
base document) by prefixing `Revision two. `. Each turn is a fresh boot, which is the agent case the tier
exists for (one long conversation, turns hours apart); the no-tier arm boots identically. Both arms build
each turn's history from the reference's per-turn record (`h<N>.json`), so the two prompts are identical by
construction, and the analysis checks the prompt token counts as well as the reply ids.

| turn | mode | prompt tokens | ON TTFT (s) | ON prefill (s) | OFF TTFT (s) | resume from | disk tokens | disk state | ids equal |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | seed | 100,040 | 44.850 | 44.843 | 44.420 | none | - | - | yes |
| 2 | append | 100,098 | **1.045** | 0.374 | 44.627 | disk | 100,033 | 1.87 GiB | yes |
| 3 | append | 100,155 | **1.041** | 0.381 | 44.593 | disk | 100,091 | 1.87 GiB | yes |
| 4 | append | 100,213 | **1.050** | 0.392 | 44.734 | disk | 100,148 | 1.87 GiB | yes |
| 5 | rewrite | 100,273 | **44.669** | 44.662 | 44.652 | none | - | - | yes |
| 6 | append | 100,331 | **1.131** | 0.388 | 45.067 | disk | 100,266 | 1.87 GiB | yes |
| 7 | append | 100,388 | **1.104** | 0.385 | 44.919 | disk | 100,324 | 1.87 GiB | yes |

The append turns resume at the previous turn's history boundary and answer in about a second, against
44.6 to 45.1 s of prefill without the tier: a saving of 43.5 to 43.9 s a turn at 100k, on identical
prompts and with identical replies (all seven id lists equal across the arms). The resumed state is about
1.87 GiB and its read is about 0.68 s at 2.8 GiB/s; the 0.37 to 0.39 s prefill is the appended text.

**The rewrite turn costs a full prefill.** Turn 5 changes the system message, so every state from turns 1
to 4 is keyed to a prefix the new prompt no longer has; `Rows.matches` refuses them all, the probe reports
`resume_from: none`, and the turn pays 44.669 s of prefill (44.662 s of it prefill) instead of the 1.05 s a
same-size append pays, a 43.6 s penalty. It then writes a fresh branch of states: all of the 100k prompt's
blocks are new because the first tokens changed. A rewrite that edits only a later message is cheaper
(window 2 measured the shallow case), but a system or base-document edit is the full price and the tier
cannot help by construction.

The per-turn store writes (the log's own write lines and the boot scans) are in `tables.md`: an append turn
writes two 113 MiB state files and no new blocks (about 226 MiB for about 1 MB of new tokens), and the
seed and rewrite turns write the full 100k row set (about 1.87 GiB of blocks) plus their state files.

**The first pass of this stage was void and is reported for the record.** Its ON arm built each turn from
the reference's FINAL conversation rather than the turn's own history, so its prompts were 100,446 tokens
for every turn 2 to 7 (348 tokens longer than the correct turn 2 and growing no further), and its turn 5
"rewrite" was a no-op because that reference history already carried the rewritten system. Its measured
resume (0.92 to 0.98 s TTFT, 0.26 to 0.30 s prefill, 1.87 GiB read) is a real 100k resume but not a
like-for-like turn; the table above is the corrected pass, whose client writes a per-turn `h<N>.json`
(`payload/agent.py`) and whose prompt token counts match the no-tier arm exactly.

K2, K3 and both K4 requests (`cmp`-equal files). The no-tier twin is the ordinary production path: within
one boot the RAM prompt cache serves turn B in 1.744 s, so the disk tier's value at 262k is across boots
(128.9 s to 1.9 s), not within one.

K2's log before the container died shows both the resume and the keeps the prompt pass wrote before the

completed turn.

## 5. The disk: what a conversation costs, the budget, and eviction

The store writes one file a 256-token block and one a kept state, under `{DIR}/{Wyhash("rows", identity)}`
(`core/rows_store.zig:140-149`); the identity binds the binary, the kernels, the checkpoint's config and
shard stamps, the tokenizer and template, and a probe's hashed bytes, so a rebuild or a checkpoint change
starts a new namespace and old states are never resumed. Blocks are content addressed and shared, so a
later state of the same prefix writes only its own file; that file's fixed part is about 113 MiB (the
DeltaNet conv windows, the recurrent states and the n-gram tail), which is why every kept state carries a
large fixed cost and why one prompt keeps two to five states.

Measured sizes and costs (this window unless noted):

| thing | measured |
|---|---|
| a 261,027-token prompt, tier on | 5 states, 5,515,339,864 B (5.14 GiB) in the identity directory; the write line's job 4,811 MiB in 4,023 ms (1.17 GiB/s) |
| the same prompt's read | 4,774 to 4,811 MiB in 1,657 to 1,716 ms (2.88 to 2.90 GiB/s) |
| a 100,013-token state | 1.87 GiB (window 3; this window's agent probes read 2,011,990,044 B) |
| a 32,013-token state | 681,530,024 B (0.63 GiB), the eviction probes |
| a 2,021-token state | 154 MB |
| an append turn at about 6k | two 113 MiB state files, 0 new blocks: 226 MiB for about 2 MB of new tokens (about 226 times) |
| the write skip line | `imprint: saving N tokens skipped (DailyBudgetSpent)` or `(OverCap)` or `(UnderFreeDiskFloor)`, `rows_write.zig:288-297,269` |

The budget knobs and their enforcement are in `SHIPPING.md` with the code citations: `--learn-gib` is the
cap under the root, enforced by `rows_store.room` which evicts other identities first and then this
identity's states by value per byte and age; `--learn-daily-gib` is the day's write ledger
(`<root>/written`, day = epoch/86400 UTC); `--learn-min-free-gib` is the filesystem floor
(`free >= floor + reserved + bytes`). The `/stats` disk block is a snapshot published when a job finishes
(`native/flashnext_host.zig:255-259`), so its `written_bytes`/`read_bytes`/`evicted` lag events that happen
between rounds; this window read the store's own boot scan ("N states, X MiB of CAP MiB") and the log's
imprint lines as the primary instruments and used `/stats` for the cap, the states and the held bytes.

**Eviction, as measured.** A 1.6 GiB cap (`--learn-gib 1.6 --learn-daily-gib 32
--learn-min-free-gib 4`) and three distinct 32k conversations (seeds 1, 5, 9). The first boot wrote all
three in turn: the write lines show A's 686 MiB of blocks and its state files, then later saves skipped
with `writing N tokens failed (Cancelled); the save is skipped` as the window stopped with jobs queued
(30 to 40 `.part` files left), and the last completed state was C's 649 MiB at 29,966 tokens. On the
restart the store's scan held one conversation's state: the probes reported A `none`, B `none`, C `disk`
at 29,967 tokens (0.635 GiB). C resumed in 1.384 s TTFT (prefill 1.165 s, 649 MiB read in 217 ms); A and
B re-prefilled cold in 13.770 s and 13.787 s. No request errored. So under a cap that holds one 32k
conversation, the store keeps one and evicts the others, a surviving state still restores from disk, and
an evicted conversation degrades to a re-prefill whose cost is the cold prefill. The exact eviction order
inside the cap (which save evicted which state) is not cleanly visible in the log; the probes and the
scan are the evidence. A first attempt at this cell used seeds 1, 8 and 15, and 1 and 15 are the same
text (the word cycle is 14), so only two conversations existed; that attempt is void and the corrected
cell above is the record.

## 6. Exactness, and what this window did and did not establish

- **The short replies are exact.** The two-token answer `[3793, 248046]` is identical in the write turn,
  the resumed turn and the no-tier twin in both kill cells, and the agent's roughly 27-token replies are
  compared turn by turn in section 2.
- **A 512-token answer is byte-equal too.** In the crash cell, the next boot resumed the long turn at
  261,046 tokens (a 6-token tail) and the no-tier twin resumed the same prompt from its RAM state at the
  shallower 2,061-token tail; the two replies' 512 token ids are identical in all 512 positions (checked
  from `K3/B.ids` and `K4/B.ids`). An earlier reading of the appended log line took the reply's own text
  for ids and called this a divergence; it was not. (The `.txt` reply files for that cell are mangled by
  a redirect collision in the harness: `oneshot` writes the reply to `OUT.txt` while the driver redirects
  its stdout to the same path. The ids, the reply JSONs and the probes are intact.) The tier's read
  returned the stored state; `TF_IMPRINT_VERIFY` is still not passed through by the recipe, so no
  read-back verification ran (`verified 0`), and that is the one exactness instrument left unused.
- **What this means for shipping**: the tier's claim must be "the same computation, resumed", and a gate
  must compare a cold full-prefill reply with a resumed reply at the SAME depth; this window's data does
  not support byte equality for arbitrary long answers across arbitrary depths. The short-answer cells
  support ids equality (the same greedy token ids, at the same prompt), which is what was compared; no cell
  in this window compares a stored state's bytes against another's, and the short-answer replies are counts
  that would survive a passage-level error.
